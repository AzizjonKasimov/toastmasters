# How I remember a speech

> Summary: The method I use: a cue card with questions, the opening and closing by heart, stage spots, and a one-week practice routine.

## Context

The goal is to tell the speech, not to read it or recite it word for word. I used this method for my Ice Breaker. The evaluator said I should rely on my notes even less, so the routine now ends with a full run with no notes.

## The method

1. **Build the speech in 4 to 6 parts,** in an order that is easy to follow. A story in time order is the easiest.
2. **Give each part a question,** like "Where did I start?" or "What changed?". On stage, I answer the question in my own words.
3. **Write down the first line of each part,** plus 2 or 3 must-say words.
4. **Learn only the opening and the closing word for word.**
5. **Give each part a spot on the stage.** Walking to the spot helps me remember what comes next.
6. **Put it all on one cue card:** part, stage spot, question, first line, must-say words.

## Practice routine (about 20 minutes a day for one week)

- **Day 1:** read the whole script out loud twice. Change any word that does not sound like me.
- **Day 2:** record myself and check the time. Make any line where I stumble simpler.
- **Day 3:** learn the opening and the closing by heart. Practice the middle from the cue card only, one part at a time: read the part once, close the script, tell it from the card, check what I missed, repeat.
- **Day 4:** a full run standing up, with the stage moves and gestures, in front of someone. Ask: "Which part was the most interesting? Where did I lose you?"
- **Day 5:** two full runs with a timer.
- **Day 6:** one full run with no notes at all.
- **Speech day:** one calm run in the morning, then rest.

## On stage

- Keep the cue card on the lectern. If I lose my place, I look down, smile, and go on.
- Slow down and pause before the key lines.
